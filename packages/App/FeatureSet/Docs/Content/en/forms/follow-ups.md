# Following Up on a Submission

With **Follow-Ups** on, the people who submit a form can follow what they sent. Each is emailed a private link to their request, where they see its state, read your team's replies, write back with files and add others who should follow it. Your team replies from the page of the incident, alert or maintenance event the form created.

## Turn follow-ups on

Open the form's **On Submit** page and switch **Follow-Ups** on. Follow-ups are on for every new form; a form made before they existed has them off until you turn them on. With follow-ups off, nothing is sent to the people who submit the form, and every link to a request stops working.

## What the person who submits gets

- **An email with their link**, as soon as they submit: to the address they gave, or on a form that asks its visitors to sign in, the address they signed in with. A submission sent without an address has nobody to follow it.
- **Their request**: its state and number, what they sent, the conversation with your team and the people following it. The thank-you page offers **Open your request** too.
- **A way to write back**: a message, files, or both. Files are held to what the form's own file questions take, and each message counts against the form's [rate limits](/docs/forms/sharing-and-security).
- **A way to add others**: each person added is emailed a link of their own. A request can have at most 20 people following it.
- **Stop following**: their link stops working, and no more emails come.

## Reply from the record's page

A record a submission created shows a **Follow-Ups** card: the form it came through, the people following and the conversation. Replying takes permission to edit the record.

- **Send Reply** emails your reply to everybody following, and keeps it as your private note on the record.
- **Add Person** emails somebody a link of their own; **Remove** stops theirs working.
- What the people following write arrives on the card and as a private note on the record, with their files, so the record's owners are told as they are of any private note.

## Who can open a request

- **On a form anybody can open**, whoever holds a person's link. Treat it as you would a password.
- **On a form that asks its visitors to sign in**, only a signed-in visitor whose address follows the request: a link alone is not enough. They also get **My requests**, every request of the form they follow.

A request is held to the form's own protections: the form must be accepting submissions, on a plan that includes forms, and opened from inside its IP allowlist.
