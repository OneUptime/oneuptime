# NIST SP 800-53 Control Mapping

How a self-hosted OneUptime supports the NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls an authorization asks about, and what stays yours. Use it to fill in your system security plan: it maps features to controls, and makes no claim of compliance or authorization, which your assessor decides for your system as a whole.

On a self-hosted install, OneUptime runs inside your boundary, under your authorization. OneUptime's own certifications cover OneUptime Cloud and the company that builds it, not your install.

:::cards
- [Access control](#access-control-ac): Accounts, roles, sign-in limits, sessions.
- [Audit and accountability](#audit-and-accountability-au): A tamper-proof trail, sent to your SIEM.
- [Identification and authentication](#identification-and-authentication-ia): Single sign-on and second factors.
- [Supply chain](#supply-chain-risk-management-sr): Signed images and chart, SBOMs, vulnerability reports.
:::

## Access control (AC)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AC-2 Account Management | Accounts created and removed from your identity provider with [SCIM](/docs/identity/scim) (Enterprise Edition); single sign-on; invitations; master admins for the instance. | The account lifecycle in your identity provider, and its reviews. |
| AC-3 Access Enforcement, AC-6 Least Privilege | Per-project roles and permissions, teams and custom roles, and API keys with permissions of their own and an expiry date. See [Permissions](/docs/permissions/index). | Granting each person and key only what it needs. |
| AC-7 Unsuccessful Logon Attempts | Sign-in, two-factor and recovery requests are rate limited per client, and wrong two-factor codes are counted per account. | Your identity provider's lockout, for single sign-on. |
| AC-12 Session Termination | [Session Length](/docs/self-hosted/session-length) (Enterprise Edition): a limit after sign-in, after time unused, or both. | Choosing the limits. |
| AC-17 Remote Access | Sign-in through your identity provider, over the TLS you terminate. | VPN and network access to the install. |

## Audit and accountability (AU)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AU-2 Event Logging, AU-3 Content of Audit Records, AU-12 Audit Record Generation | [Audit Logs](/docs/configuration/audit-logs) (Enterprise Edition): each entry records the user, API key or workflow that acted, the action, the resource and the change. | Turning them on in each project. |
| AU-6 Audit Record Review, AU-9 Protection of Audit Information, AU-11 Audit Record Retention | Nobody can edit or delete an entry; each is kept for 7 to 180 days. [Audit Log Export](/docs/configuration/audit-log-export) sends entries as they are recorded to S3-compatible storage, Google Cloud Storage, Azure Blob Storage, a webhook or a syslog receiver. | Review in your SIEM, and retention beyond 180 days. |

## Configuration management (CM)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CM-2 Baseline Configuration, CM-6 Configuration Settings | The install is a Helm chart and its `values.yaml`; monitors, alerts and other resources can be managed as code with the [Terraform provider](/docs/terraform/index). | Keeping both in version control, under change control. |
| CM-7 Least Functionality | Optional components (PgBouncer, vLLM, the database operators) are off until turned on, every integration is off until set up, and the update check and check-in can be turned off. | Turning on only what you use. See the [Hardening Guide](/docs/self-hosted/hardening). |

## Contingency planning (CP)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CP-9 System Backup, CP-10 System Recovery and Reconstitution | The Enterprise Edition backs up PostgreSQL and ClickHouse to S3-compatible storage you run, and restores from it: see [Backups](/docs/installation/backups). | Backup schedules, volume snapshots, tested restores, and your recovery objectives. |

## Identification and authentication (IA)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IA-2 Identification and Authentication | Single sign-on with SAML or OpenID Connect in every edition ([SSO](/docs/identity/sso), [Global SSO](/docs/identity/global-sso)), with **Require SSO for Login** for the whole server; an authenticator app or a security key as a second factor for password sign-in. | Multi-factor authentication and PIV or CAC credentials, enforced in your identity provider. |
| IA-5 Authenticator Management | Passwords are stored as salted scrypt hashes; API keys expire on the date you set, and can be deleted at any time. | Password and key policies, and rotation. |

## Incident response (IR)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IR-4 Incident Handling, IR-5 Incident Monitoring, IR-6 Incident Reporting | [Incidents](/docs/incidents/index), [on-call](/docs/on-call/index) schedules and escalation, [status pages](/docs/status-pages/index) and [postmortems](/docs/incidents/postmortems), for the incidents of the systems OneUptime monitors. | Your incident response plan, and reporting to your authorities. |

## Risk assessment and system acquisition (RA, SA)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RA-5 Vulnerability Monitoring and Scanning | Each release attaches a Grype vulnerability report for each image's SBOM: see [Supply Chain Security](/docs/self-hosted/supply-chain-security#vulnerability-reports). | Scanning what you run on a schedule, and deciding what to fix first. |
| SA-11 Developer Testing and Evaluation | OneUptime's source is public; CodeQL scans it every night and its dependencies are audited every week. | Your own review of the release you run. |

## System and communications protection (SC)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SC-7 Boundary Protection | With the [air-gapped values](/docs/installation/air-gapped#install-from-your-registry), no connection out of your network on its own; settings for what monitors, webhooks and workflows may reach. | Network policies and firewalls. |
| SC-8 Transmission Confidentiality and Integrity | Uses the TLS you terminate for the install's address and status pages. | TLS at your load balancer or ingress. |
| SC-13 Cryptographic Protection | OneUptime's images are not FIPS 140-validated builds: see [Cryptography and FIPS 140](/docs/self-hosted/hardening#cryptography-and-fips-140). | Validated modules where your boundary requires them. |
| SC-28 Protection of Information at Rest | Secrets taken from Kubernetes secrets you create. | Encrypting the databases' volumes. |

## System and information integrity (SI)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SI-2 Flaw Remediation | Security fixes ship in releases, and advisories are published on GitHub; the [update check](/docs/self-hosted/update-checks) tells master admins when one affects the version they run, from a mirror inside the gap. | Upgrading on your schedule. |
| SI-4 System Monitoring | Monitors and probes inside your network, logs, metrics and traces, and alerts on them: what OneUptime is for. | What to monitor, and who is told. |

## Supply chain risk management (SR)

| Control | What OneUptime provides | What stays yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SR-3 Supply Chain Controls and Processes, SR-4 Provenance, SR-11 Component Authenticity | Every image signed with Sigstore cosign; the Helm chart signed with OneUptime's key; SPDX SBOM and SLSA provenance attestations on each image; CycloneDX SBOMs and vulnerability reports on each release; a checksum for every file of an air-gapped bundle. See [Supply Chain Security](/docs/self-hosted/supply-chain-security). | Verifying each before it runs. |
